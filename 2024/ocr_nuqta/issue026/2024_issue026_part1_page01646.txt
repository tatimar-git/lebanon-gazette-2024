- Safety pins

- Tweezers
Take the following general steps in case of injury:
- Place your hand in front of the injured person mouth to check the breathing. Place two fingers on the injured person neck below the ear to check heartbeats (normal is 60-100 beats per minute). If necessary, begin rescue breathing and Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
- If the person's breathing and heart rate are normal but the person is unconscious, treat the person as if there is a spinal injury. Stabilize the head and neck by placing your hands on both sides of the person's head. Keep the head in line with the spine and prevent movement. Call for medical help
- Stop any bleeding by firmly pressing a clean cloth on the wound. If the injury is serious, be careful not to move the person's head. If blood soaks through the cloth, do not remove it. Place another cloth over the first one. Call for medical help
- If you suspect a skull fracture, do not apply direct pressure to the bleeding site, and do not remove any debris from the wound. Cover the wound with sterile gauze dressing. Call for medical help
- If the person is vomiting, prevent choking by rolling the person's head, neck, and body as one unit onto their side. This still protects the spine, which you must always assume is injured in the case of a head injury. Call for medical help
- If there was an eye injury or debris in the eye, wash your hands thoroughly and pour water over the open eye of the injured person. Do not rub the eyes for it might cause serious cornea injuries
- Apply ice packs to swollen areas
Follow these precautions:
- Do NOT wash a head wound that is deep or bleeding a lot
- Do NOT remove any object sticking out of a wound
- Do NOT move the person unless necessary
- Do NOT shake the person if he or she seems dazed
Interpret technical drawings and blue prints
- Hand sketches the primary view:
- Understand the needs and requests of your customer
- Visualize the project that need to be done
- Draw the parts of the project from top to bottom
- Draw the back parts of the project smaller than the front parts to make it look like in 3D
- Color where you would visualize shadows, typically on bottom and back parts of the drawing
- Refer to a woodworking plan before beginning a project:
- Refer to the drawing scale typically written under the title block
- If scale is not indicated, use a scale ruler on the drawn dimensions to determine drawing scale
- Calculate real-scale dimensions after determining drawing scale
- Note down all the primary views of the drawing (plan, elevation, section)
- Refer to the cut list and visualize each piece of the project
- Puzzle up the pieces visualized in the most fit way possible
