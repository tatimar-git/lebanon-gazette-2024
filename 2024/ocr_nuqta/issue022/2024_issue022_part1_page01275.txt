- Brining is the process of soaking food in a solution of water and salt, and possibly herbs and spices in order to preserve, tenderize or flavour various food or cuts of foods.
- Lean meats such as chicken, pork, turkey, fish and shrima are considered good foods to brine.
- It is not necessary to brine red meats since they can benefit more from a marinade.
- Brining changes the meat's physical nature. The salt breaks down the structure of the muscle, which allows more liquid to enter the meal and thus retaining more moisture.
* Brining also adds flavour, seasoning from the inside out.
Brining time

Meat | Time needed
Shrimps or scallops | 15 to 30 minutes
Fish fillets | 10 to 30 minutes
Whole fish | 1 to 3 hours
Boneless chicken pieces | 30 minutes
Bone-in chicken pieces | 1 to 2 hours
Whole chicken | 4 to 12 hours
Turkey breast | 4 to 12 hours
Whole turkey | 12 to 24 hours
Boneless pork chops | 30 minutes
Bone-in pork chops | 30 minutes to 1 hour
Simple brining recipe
* 4 cups of water
* 1/4 cup of kosher salt
* 1/2 cup of brown sugar or sweetener (honey, molasses, or brown sugar) can be added if sweetening is desired
* Any additional herbs, spices or aromatic ingredients is optional
* Use fine salt if you want to prepare a cold brine
Brining Whole Chicken
* Mix the salt and brown sugar (or honey) if desired with half of the water required in a non-reactive stockpot
* Place the pot on high heat and allow the mixture to dissolve by stirring periodically
* Add herbs, spices or aromatic ingredients
* Turn off the heat
* Add ice to cool down the brine faster
* Pour the brine in a food grade container
* Add the whole chicken and try to keep it submerge at all times by placing something heavy on top of it
* Seal the container
* Refrigerate for 4 to 12 hours
