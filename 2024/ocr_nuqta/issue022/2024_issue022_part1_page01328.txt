Sugaring is a food preservation method similar to pickling and curing. Sugaring creates an environment hostile to microbial life and thus preserving the food from spoilage.
This process is done mainly with any table sugar in crystallized form. Food can either be dried before sugaring or glazed in sugar syrup.
Fruits (apples, pears, cherry, plum, apricots and peaches), vegetables (ginger and carrots), Angelica, and citrus peel are all famous foods preserved through the sugaring process.
1- Sugared lemon slices
- Wash and dry lemons
- Sterilize the jars
- Slice the lemon thinly
- Remove the seeds
- Transfer lemon into a mixing bowl
- Add the sugar in a ratio of 1:1 (equal quantities)
- Combine ingredients well together with a salad tongs
- Place sugared lemon in a jar
- Add a tablespoon of the sugar syrup you have from in the mixing bowl
- Proceed with placing lemons in the jar and sugaring them until the jar is full
- Wipe clean the jar with a wet paper towel
- Cover the jar with cling film
- Seal the jar
- Refrigerate for 2-3 days before using them
N.B: Sugared lemons can be preserved between 6 and 12 months.
