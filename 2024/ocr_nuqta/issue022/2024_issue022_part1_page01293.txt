It is a convenient and readily available drying method with good results. It takes around 5 to 6 hours, sometimes even more, depending on the type of oven and what food to dry. Meat and starchy food take longer time to dry. Oven temperature should be very low, 60°C if possible, because a higher temperature will more likely cook the food instead of drying it cut.
Air circulation is very important during oven drying. Keep the oven door slightly open during the drying process; a gap of 5 to 10 cm wide is enough. Placing a fan near the gap can also help in air circulation.
Any type of oven can be used, but keep in mind that the electric oven consumes a lot of energy.
The most economic oven to use is the convection oven. They have controllable temperature settings with the lowest at 49°C.
Pros
- No need to buy drying equipment
- Multi-functional, the oven can be used all year-round for other purposes, and doesn't take useful countertop space
Cons
- Takes longer time to dry food, twice the time needed in an electric dehydrator
- Requires frequent checking and fiddling during the drying process
- Consumes more energy
- More difficult to maintain temperature and keep good air circulation
Higher risk of burning or under-drying food
1- Drying apples
- Set the oven at 90°C
- Wash and dry the apples
- Remove the centre of the apple using a corer
- Peel the skin off
- Slice the apple into 3 mm slices with a mandoline slicer
- Combine water and white vinegar (4 cups of water with 2 tb of white vinegar)
image here
- Soak the apple slices for 30 minutes to prevent it from oxidation and browning during the drying process
- Drain the apples by laying the slices on a clean kitchen towel
- Place another towel over the slices and pat it dry
- Arrange the slice on perforated trays lined up with parchment paper (or stackable trays)
- Bake the slices for 1.5 to 2 hours in the oven with the door slightly open
- Continue drying longer if you want it to be crispier
- Let it cool down before storing it in airtight containers
