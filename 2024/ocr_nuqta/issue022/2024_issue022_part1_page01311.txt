It is a process of oxidation in which alcohol is converted into acetic acid by the agency of bacteria. Apple cider vinegar, wine vinegar, palm wine vinegar, coconut water vinegar and kambucha are famous acetic fermented foods.
The production of vinegar involves two fermentation processes. The first utilizing yeast to produce alcohol from sugar. The second utilizing acetic acid bacteria to oxidize alcohol into acetic acid.
Vinegar consists of 4 grams of acetic acid per 100 ml.
Apple cider vinegar
- Wash, dry, quarter and core the apples
- Cut into small chunks
- Place apple chunks in a glass jar and fill it to the shoulder
- Add sugar and water
- Use a stick to consolidate the apple pieces
- Cover the jar with cheesecloth and rubber band
- Leave it undisturbed to ferment for 7 days
- Push down the top pieces once a day from day 2 to day 7 and let the bottom ones to come up. This step is important to prevent the growth of bad mould on the apples
- Cover back and put it back to continue fermenting
- Repeat the same process everyday from day 8 to day 14
- Check for bubbles between days 15 to 22. You should see a lot of bubbles. At that stage you should smell a lot of alcohol
- Keep on repeating the same process of pushing down to slices every 2 to 3 days from day 23 to day 30. At this stage you will notice that the bubbles have subsided and are calm
- Continue repeating the process every 2 to 3 days
- Strain the ingredients on day 31 or 32 in a non-metallic strainer. At this point the first fermentation is completed.
- Transfer the liquid into a glass jar
- Cover lightly with a lid
- Let it ferment undisturbed for another month
- Open the jar 1 month after the second fermentation. It smells like vinegar
- Place a cheesacloth into a strainer
- Strain it gently through layers of cheesecloth to have a clear vinegar
- Transfer the vinegar to a glass bottle. It is now ready to use after 2 months of fermentation
N.B: The colour will get darker as the vinegar ages.
