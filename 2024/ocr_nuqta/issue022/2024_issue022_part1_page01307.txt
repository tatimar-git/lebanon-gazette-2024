Fermentation is a metabolic process in which microorganisms' activity creates desirable change in food and beverages, whether it's increasing flavour, preserving foodstuffs, providing health benefits, or more.
- Lacto fermentation is the process by which yeast strains and bacteria convert starches or sugar into lactic acid. Lactic acid bacteria are vital to producing and preserving inexpensive, sourdough bread, wholesome foods such as sauerkraut, yogurt, kefir, and pickles.
- The simplest method of lacto fermentation is to submerge a food that naturally contains lactic acid bacteria, such as cabbage and cucumber, into a brine of water and salt. OR The simplest method of lacto fermentation is to mix a food that naturally contains lactic acid bacteria, with a certain percentage of salt.
- Fermented milk, yogurt and sourdough may also ferment on their own, but often a starter culture is used to ensure safety and consistency of flavour.
- A sealed container, such as a glass jar, ceramic crock, or food-grade plastic container, is typically used to limit oxygen exposure.

- As bacteria break down sugar, lactic acid and carbon dioxide are formed, removing oxygen and making the food more acidic. This encourages the growth of even more lactic acid bacteria and prevents the growth of other microorganisms.
- The time food takes to ferment ranges from days to months. Afterward, the fermented food is usually stored in a cool place to slow any further fermentation and prevent spoilage.
1- Sourdough
It is one of the oldest forms of grain fermentation. Most breads use commercial baker's yeast to help the dough rise. However, sourdough is naturally leavened by wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria that are naturally present and lactic acid bacteria in flour.
- Begin by making the sourdough starter
- Combine flour and water in a mixing bowl as 100% hydration (equal amounts of water and flour)
- Mix ingredients with a whisk until flour is totally incorporated with water
- Cover loosely the bowl with a lid
- Let the mixture sit undisturbed for 2 days at a temperature of 21°C to 26°C away from direct sunlight
- Remove the lid after 2 days to feed the sourdough starter
- Remove half the quantity of the starter
- Add again same amount of flour and water as done before
- Mix again with the whisk to fully incorporate the flour with water
- Cover the bowl loosely again with the lid and place the bowl in the same conditions as before
- Feed the starter every 12 hours for around 7 days or more depending on the type of flour
- Feed every 8 hours if by day 7 bubbling is very slow
- Stop the feeding process when the starter is frothy with bubbles
- Refrigerate sourdough or dehydrate it if you are not planning to use it soon
- Do not leave ready sourdough on the counter without feeding it
2- Yogurt
- Put the milk in a pot or sauce pan (2 litres)
- Bring it to boil over medium heat until it reaches 82°C
- Stir often to prevent having a film in the bottom of the pan
- Check temperature with a digital thermometer
- Turn off the heater and let milk cool down until it reaches a temperature of 43°C
- Put a quarter of cup ready yogurt in a bowl preferably at room temperature
- Pour over the milk
- Mix well milk and ready yogurt
- Cover the bowl with cling film
- Poke some holes into the cling film with a fork for better airflow
- Place the bowl in warm environment at around 32°C for 5 to8 hours (you can place it in the oven)
- Keep yogurt refrigerated
