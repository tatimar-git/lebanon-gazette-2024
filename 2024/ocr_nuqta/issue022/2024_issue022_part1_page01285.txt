الجريدة الرسمية - العدد ٢٢ - ٢٠٢٤/٥/٣٠
١٢٨٥
It is the oldest and most readily available food drying method. It's free and does not require costly electricity or gas energy. However, in order to be effective, there should be long hours of direct sun exposure which is not available all times. In addition, the humidity should be below 60% with a temperature of approximately 30°C.
Pros
- Relatively low cost
- No added cost for equipment
- No added cost for electricity or gas
- Environmentally friendly
Cons
- Nearly impossible to control temperature and humidity to ensure consistent drying conditions
- Unpredictable results, some foods may not dry uniformly
- It is a long process that might take several days to achieve desired results
- It is labour intensive since food should be sun dried during the day and brought back in during the night due to the risk of moisture
- It is a risky preservation method as it involves heavy reliance on unpredictable weather conditions
1) Drying herbs
- Wash the herbs to remove soil and other debris
- Spin the herbs dry in a salad spinner
- Lay them on a clean towel for an hour to remove excess water
- Place the herbs on a perforated tray
- Place the tray in direct sunlight during the day and bring it inside during the night
- Check the herbs the following day, it should have dried nicely. You will know when it's ready when there is a crunch
- Put the dried herbs in a zip lock plastic bag
- Seal the bag
- Let out most of the air in the bag. This will also make it easier to break up the dried herbs
- Take out any stringy bits of the leaves
- Store it in an airtight jar
- Close the jar very well
2) Drying tomatoes
- Select plum tomatoes that have a dense meat and high sugar content
- Wash the tomatoes and dry them well
- Cut the tomatoes in half lengthwise
- Place the tomatoes on a screen tray cut side up or on a baking tray lined with parchment paper
- Sprinkle tomatoes with salt
- Place the tray in direct sunlight during the day and bring it inside during the night
- Keep the trays in the sun for 4 days (sometimes more depending on the weather conditions)
- Check the tomatoes to make sure that it is well dry from the inside as well as outside
- Soak the dried tomatoes in white vinegar for 30 minutes to remove the salt
- Put the jars in a stockpot filled with water and bring it to boil for 20 minutes in order to pasteurize it
- Put the parsley, sliced garlic, slice of chili pepper and capers in the base of the jar
- Squeeze the tomatoes to remove excess vinegar
- Place the squeezed tomatoes in the jar
- Add the ingredients when you are at the half of the jar
- Keep on adding more tomatoes
- Cover the top with a layer of the ingredients
- Add olive oil until the top of the jar
- Spread the olive oil in the jar with a fork until the bubbles disappear
