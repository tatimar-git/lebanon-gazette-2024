Identify risks associated with cleaning chemicals and take preventive measures
Potential hazards
- Skin irritation from using soaps and detergents
- Infections: broken skin can be infected or burned from hazardous chemicals
- Burns: oven cleaners, drain openers and grill cleaners can cause burns to the skin and eyes as well as respiratory damage
image here
Respiratory harm: Chlorine and ammonia, common commercial kitchen chemicals, can cause respiratory, skin and eye irritation and death if mixed together.
Headaches
Latex allergies: some workers may be allergic to latex. Use non-latex gloves to prevent any allergic reactions
Chemical contamination
Preventive measures:
Use eco-friendly low or non-toxic cleaners
Provide a thorough and regularly reinforced training program that includes emergency procedure training
Use high quality PPE (gloves, aprons, eye protection)
Mix chemicals to recommended concentrations
Never mix chlorine and ammonia
Read the labels and familiarize yourself with the Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) which provide more in-depth handling information
Label cleaning bottles clearly indicating what cleaning solution is in the bottle
Use lower shelves to store chemicals to avoid the risk of spills
Store chemicals away from food
Do not store chemicals in bottles meant for food and vice versa
Clean and sanitize kitchen tools and food contact surfaces before preparing meal ingredients
- Train employees on how to use chemicals
- Hire a reputable pest control company
- Wear PPE before cleaning
- Remove any food before cleaning and sanitizing
- Wipe dirt of food preparation counters with a wiping cloth
- Wash with hot water and correct amount of detergent
- Rinse with clean hot water
- Apply sanitizer and wipe it off with a single-use paper towel
- Wash utensils, cutting boards, pots and pans and allow them to air-dry
- All other food contact surfaces must be cleared and sanitized between uses
