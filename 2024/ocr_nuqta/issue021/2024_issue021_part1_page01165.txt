Cross-contamination is the accidental transfer of contaminants from one surface to another.
Common causes are:
- Food-to-Food: it occurs in the refrigerator when meats stored on top shelves drip onto raw or cooked food placed on lower shelves
- People-to-food: It occurs when touching raw meats then handling food without washing hands between tasks, or when wiping hands with a soiled apron or towel, or when failing to change gloves when handling different foods, and when personal hygiene practices are not respected.
- Equipment-to-food: It occurs when using unclean slicers and utensils to prepare food, also when using the same cutting boards, knives, work surfaces and utensils for both raw and cooked foods, and when storing food in unclean containers.
Preventive measures include:
Maintain high standards of personal hygiene
Dispose of waste properly
Use separate equipment and utensils to prepare raw and cooked foods
Rinse fresh vegetables in running water to remove visible dirt
Remove and discard the outermost leaves of a head of lettuce and cabbage
Avoid leaving cut vegetables at room temperature for many hours
Wash and sanitize food contact surfaces between uses, when switching from raw meats to vegetables, every 4 hours during continuous use, and after final use of the day
Cover and store raw food on shelves below cooked and ready-to-eat food in the refrigerator
Store cut food properly by keeping it covered and separating ready-to-eat from raw food
Prepare only one type of food at a time then wash and sanitize food contact surfaces before using them with a different food
Designate certain equipment for use with specific types of food only, such as using one slicer for vegetables and the other for meats
Use colour coded cutting boards: green for vegetables and fruits, yellow for raw poultry, blue for cooked food, white for dairy products, tan for fish and seafood, and red for raw meats
