• Discard any rotten vegetables and remove damaged portions
• Soak vegetables in tap water for about 15 minutes
• Rinse vegetables thoroughly under running tap water
• Peel off the skin if you are not planning to eat it to further reduce the microbial load (not true)
• Rinse the peeled vegetable under running tap water
• Dry vegetables with a paper towel
• Use a soft brush to clean firm produce such as potatoes, carrots and turnips.
• Remove the outer layer of leafy greens like lettuce, spinach, Brussels sprouts, etc.
• Submerge it in cool water and swish
• Drain or use a vegetable spinner
• Pat dry with paper towel
Organize appropriate cutting tools
- Secure the designated cutting board with a damp cloth
- Remove knives from knife bag or rack and place them on a tray
- Sharpen the knife with a sharpening steel
- Hold the honing steel tip up at 45 degree
- Place the edge of the knife to rest on the steel at the desired angle
- Pull the knife at 22 degree 2 to 4 times across and up along the steel
- Go under at same angle and repeat again
- Clean the knife before using it
- Prepare the vegetable peeler
- Prepare the mandolin
- Prepare the grater
- Prepare waste bowls and bowls for cut food

For teaching purposes, wearing gloves is not advisable for the following reasons: • To maintain high hygiene practices regarding nails cut and cleanliness, removal of jewelleries and accessories, and frequent hands washing • To see the position of fingers when cutting to prevent cuts hazards • To have a firmer grip when cutting After trainees have mastered the cutting techniques, and they will start working in a production facility, wearing gloves is mandatory to prevent cross-contamination.
