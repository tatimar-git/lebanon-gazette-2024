Lifting injuries
Handling deliveries, moving crates and trays can cause muscle strains and skeletal damage.
- Use proper storage practices to avoid overreaching or bending
- Store heavy items on the middle shelves
- Follow the proper lifting technique
- Empty garbage containers before they become completely full
- Use dollies or carts whenever possible to move heavy objects
- Seek help from others when lifting a heavy load
- Avoid lifting items that are slippery, hot or unevenly balanced

Slips, trips and falls hazards
Wet and slippery surfaces, uneven floor, and obstructions in walkways are among the causes of slips, trips and falls accidents.
- Block access to wet areas and use warning signs
- Clean spills immediately
- Clean all floors regularly
- Wear non-slip footwear
- Keep walkways and aisles clear of boxes
- Announce your presence in blind corners
- Avoid standing in front of swinging doors and doorways
- Avoid running in the kitchen
- Install non-slip floor covering in wet or spill areas
- Report any potential hazards to management

Burns and scalds hazards
Direct flame, hot surfaces, handling hot pots and working with hot oil all provide the opportunity to inflict burn injuries.
- Use oven mitts or dry cloths when handling hot objects
- Open the lid away from your body when checking the contents of a hot pot
- Put food into the pan with a motion away from your body
- Turn pot handles inward to avoid accidental spillage by passers-by
- Turn pot handles away from heat sources
Announce your presence if you are coming up behind someone with a hot pot
Tell others when you are resting hot pans
Do not overheat oil, it can burst into flames
Turn off the gas in case of an oil fire and cover the flames with a fire blanket or damp cloth or use a chemical fire extinguisher if it is a large fire
Never use water to extinguish an oil fire
Don't carry a burning pan
Use a trolley to carry hot liquids
