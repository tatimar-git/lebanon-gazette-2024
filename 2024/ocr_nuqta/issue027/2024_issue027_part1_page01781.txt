Prepare the drill machine:
- Place the ring for quick adjustment of depth under the casing of the spring
- Insert the spindle into the doweling jig and lower the drill onto the workpiece
- Set the depth with the stop ring on the shaft of the spindle: set the depth to 4.1 cm for the end grain edges and 1.2 cm for the surfaces, the depth of the dowel holes is slightly deeper by 0.1 cm than the dowel length to create a little gap for excess glue
- Use an Allen key to release the worm on the ring
- Lower the ring onto the desired depth secure the worm properly
- The depth is now set by the ring and the spring casing
- Make sure the drilling machine is unplugged
- Fix a 0.6 mm self centered bit to the drill machine
- Fix a stop collar to the drill bit to set the drilling depth of the hole at 4.1 cm: the depth of the dowel holes is slightly deeper by 0.1 cm than the dowel length to create a little gap for excess glue
Drill dowel holes:
- Fit your drill with the spindle and drill out the all the marked holes
- Start drilling until you reach the set depth
- Drill holes in both halves of the joint
- Repeat the process to all the marked dowels
- Dry fit 0.6 cm diameter dowels into holes as you go to make sure of the fit: If necessary, tidy the ends of the dowels by rubbing it with some fine grit sandpaper
Glue dowels into the rails and stiles and slats:
- Apply a layer of glue before inserting the dowels inside the rails
- Place the dowels in the drilled-out holes
- Try to join the pieces together when the glue has set, without glue on the other doveled side to make sure the fit is right again
- Apply glue into the holes of of the stiles and slats
- Join the rails to stiles and slats
- Use a mallet to lightly drive the pieces in
- Secure them with a clamp: you will get one back board, one front board and two side boards
- Let dry over night
Apply finishing technique: sanding and color painting
Prepare the wood:
Apply wood filler to any wide or deep cracks in your MDF such as pilot holes:
- Use one end of a craft stick to scoop the wood filler out of the pot and smooth it into the crack or hole
- Use the other edge of the craft stick to scrape across the wood filler, making it level with the surface of the wood
- Let the wood filler dry for time that the manufacturer suggests: increase the drying time by 30 to 60 minutes in winter and cold weather
