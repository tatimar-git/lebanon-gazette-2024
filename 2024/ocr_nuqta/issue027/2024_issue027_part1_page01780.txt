Make the cut:
- Use a push shoe, push stick, or splitter when cutting thin boards
- Let the blade come to a full stop before raising the blade, to prevent removing a small amount of wood which can change the length of our cut
- Measure the work piece after cutting
- Make sure pieces are square and cut at perfect 90-degree angles
- Adjust the cut if necessary
- Pull back the board and turn off the saw
- Label the cut pieces as you go to make it easier to assemble later
- Check all cut pieces for square by measuring from the top right corner across the piece to the lower left corner
image here
- Note the measurement. Then measure the opposite two corners. If the measurements are the same, your piece is square
- Sand lightly by hand pieces' edges if needed using a 320 to 400 grit sandpaper
- Put all the cut boards aside
- Store disposable scrap pieces in designated buckets
Prepare and apply the joining technique for the bed frame: dowel joints
image here
Mark the dowels positions to joint stiles and rails:
- Set the stiles and rails together edge to edge using a spirit level: make sure edges are flush
- Locate the dowel holes 2 cm away from the stile's edges using a pencil and a square
- Draw lines across the joint to mark the dowel positions using a pencil and a square: make sure to draw on the inside side of the boards
- Repeat the same process to all the stiles and rails joined together
Mark the dowels positions to joint sides rails to legs:
- Locate 2 dowel holes on the top and bottom rails 2cm away from the rail's edges, using a pencil and a square from 2 sides
- Locate 2 dowel holes on the top legs 2 cm and 4 cm away from the top legs' edges from one side
- Locate 2 dowel holes on the bottom legs 7 cm and 11 cm away from the bottom legs' edges from one side
- Mark the holes positions to joint legs to front and back rails
- Mark 1 holes on the top legs moving 4 cm away from the top edges
- Mark 1 holes on the bottom legs moving 9 cm away from the bottom legs
Clamp a dowel drill into the boring spindle:
- Use the two worm screws on the chuck to properly clamp the drill
- Securely screw in both worms to clamp the drill properly
Clamp the doweling jig to the workpieces to be doweled:
- Clamp the doweling jig onto your workpiece: the workpiece must touch the front slop of the jig and the holes in the jig must be placed on the edges and inner surface of the workpiece
- Make sure to clamp the jig along its whole length
- Let the jig's drilling registration mark aligns with the dowel layout line: if the jig has interchangeable bit sleeves, thread in the appropriate sleeve for the bit you'll use
