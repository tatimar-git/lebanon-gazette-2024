Steaming vegetables in a steamer basket
- Wash and cut vegetables at the same size in order to cook quickly and evenly
- Pour around 2 cm of water into the steamer
- Put the basket compartment into the base of the steamer. The water should not enter into the basket compartment
- Cover the steamer
- Pre-heat the steamer over medium heat
- Place the vegetables in the basket compartment once you notice steam coming out of the steamer
- Make sure to leave space between the vegetables without piling them
- Cover the steamer and make sure it is airtight
- Lower the gas to the minimum level
- Leave the vegetables to cook for a few minutes
- Check if the vegetables are cooked well by piercing them with a knife or fork
- Turn the gas off and remove the basket

Steam food in the microwave
Steaming vegetables
- Prepare the vegetables
- Place the vegetables in a heat-resistant bowl
- Seal with plastic wrap
- Cook in the microwave for 3 to 5 minutes
- Open the microwave and puncture the plastic wrap
- Unwrap carefully
Steam food in a paper bag
Paper bag cookery involves the cooking of food in an oven using paper bags which allow cooking in steam and hot air generated by its own juices. It is also referred to cooking en papillote. Fish, poultry and various meats are usually paper steamed.
Steam fish fillet in a paper bag
- Preheat the oven at 200 °C (400 °F)
- Slice the lemon into thin slices
- Season the fish with salt and pepper
- Place the fillet on a parchment paper
- Place 2 slices of lemon on each fillet along with 2 sprigs of thyme and 2 tablespoons of butter
- Brush the outside of the parchment paper with slightly beaten white eggs
- Add 1/4 cup of white wine
- Fold the paper in half and seal the edges
- Start folding from one corner
- Start the next fold in the middle of the first fold and so on all the way around the fish
- Transfer the fish to the oven on a baking tray
- Bake for 10-12 minutes depending on the thickness of the fish (every 2.5 cm thickness the cooking time is 10 minutes at 200 °C)
