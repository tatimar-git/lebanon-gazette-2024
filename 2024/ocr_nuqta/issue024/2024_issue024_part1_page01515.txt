2- Types of fat to use
Choosing the right oil depends on a number of factors including the cooking technique, healthfulness of the recipe, desired flavour and type of cuisine.
As a rule, the best oils for frying are neutral flavour oils with a high smoke-point (the temperature at which an oil starts to burn).
Most popular forms of fat and oil used for frying include:
- Shortening
- Peanut oil
- Sunflower oil
- Canola oil
- Soybean oil
- Rendered animal fat such as lard
3- Tips for better frying
- Monitor the temperature: you can use a thermometer to ensure that the oil has reached the correct temperature.
- Don't crowd: Fry in small batches to avoid major fluctuations in temperature.
- Cut food in similar sizes: This will guarantee even frying at the same speed.
- Drain thoroughly: Drain fried foods on a rack or paper towels to get rid of excess oil.
- Season immediately: Season the food as soon as it comes out of the fryer since the flavour is locked when the food is still hot.
- Consume fried foods hot: Fried foods should be eaten hot in order to enjoy the crunchy exterior and moist interior. When cold, fried foods become soggy.
- Safety first: Never leave a pot of frying oil unattended. If you are using a pan to fry, use a large sturdy pan with enough empty space and a heavy-duty slotted spoon or tongs to avoid contact with the oil
Deep frying is a dry-heat cooking method that utilizes oil or fat to cook pieces of food. The process works by completely submerging food in hot oil. Depending on the type of oil chosen and food being fried, a high temperature of up to 204°C is maintained to create golden-brown surface textures in a short amount of time
Deep-frying French fries
- Wash and peel off the potatoes skin
- Cut off one side of the potato in order to flatten it
- Slice the potato with the mandclin French fries blade
- Use a cloth to hold the potato during slicing
- Soak cut potatoes immediately in ice water for 2-3 hours to remove starch and make them crispier (this will depend on the variety of potato chosen)
- Heat oil until it reaches 150°
- Dry the potatoes using a cotton cloth (or in a colander)
- Fry the potatoes in a deep-fryer or in a deep pot for 3 minutes. This is called blanching
- Remove the blanched potatoes and place them on a paper lined baking tray
- Fry again the blanched potatoes at 180°C until they become crispy with a golden colour
- Drain the fries on a paper lined tray
- Salt them immediately with kosher salt
N.B: Food can be fired in a commercial deep fryer, or a countertop single unit fryer, or a deep frying pot, or a deep stockpot
Pan-frying is a method cooking that uses a heavy pan (skillet or sauté pan) containing a small amount of fat. Food is cooked over moderate heat and should be turned at least once to cook both sides.
The fat which helps to prevent the fried food from sticking to the pan, is heated until it is very hot before food is added to the pan.
This method is ideal for cooking fish filets, eggplants, thin cut pork chops, breaded chicken breast and other types of lean meats.
