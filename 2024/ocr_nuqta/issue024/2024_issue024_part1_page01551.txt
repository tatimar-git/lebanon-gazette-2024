Rotisserie, also known as spit-roasting or grilling on a skewer.
Rotisserie cooks by roasting meat on a spit or long rod over indirect heat. The spit slowly turns so the meat cooks evenly – and gets the perfect sear while locking in flavour. The rotisserie needs to turn at a constant speed in order to cook correctly.
When using this method, the meat does not grill, it roasts as it cooks and absorbs a smoke flavour while developing a perfectly crisp crust.
Rotisserie grilling or cooking makes food moist because it seals in the natural juices and flavours, and it sears quickly.
This method is perfect for cooking large cuts of meat and whole animals like pigs, chicken and turkey.
1- Cook rotisserie chicken
- Preheat the rotissoire to 176°C - 205°C
- Pat dry the chicken with a paper towel
- Truss the chicken as discussed in Competence unit 5 (page 86)
- Mount the chicken on the spit
- Place the spit through the bottom of the chicken cavity and onto the fork already placed
- Take another fork and slide it up in the opposite direction
- Place the spit over a large bowl
- Brush the chicken with olive oil, pepper and salt
- Mount the spit on the rotisserie motor in a preheated barbecue grill
- Place the spit across the grill
- Start the motor up
- Lower the hood
- Let the chicken cook for 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 and a half hour depending on the chicken size and its quality
- Open the hood during the last 10 minutes and check the internal temperature of the chicken. It should be 74°C
- Stop the motor and remove the spit
- Put the chicken on a cutting board
- Loosen the fork screw
- Slide the fork off the chicken then slide off the chicken
- Carve and serve
